I miss thinking. AI didn't take it. The hype did.

Not the performative kind: the gymnastics, the abstractions nobody questioned. Not problem-solving either — the slow kind, the kind that needs your full weight on it before it moves. Agentic coding made me a little faster, so I opened eight sessions. The writing moved to the machine. The thinking didn't. It still grows the way it always did, with your full attention.

A year ago I went all in on AI coding agents. Spent thousands on tokens. Built tools, rebuilt them as the ecosystem shifted under me. Everything I could produce expanded overnight.

Then I noticed: my output expanded, but the energy didn't move where I expected.

<SectionDivider spacing="subsection" />

Writing got cheap. That part worked. Steering AI, <span class="aside">framing problems, reviewing output, catching the plausible-but-wrong</span>, takes effort, but it's learnable. The part I didn't anticipate: thinking didn't shrink. It expanded. Because when creation is free, the cost that used to filter what should exist disappears. Every feature too expensive to build, every doc not worth writing, every edge case too unlikely to justify. They all show up. And you're still the accountable maintainer.

<SectionDivider spacing="subsection" />

When the 2am page comes, you're searching through generated noise for the signal that matters. You open a file and nothing looks familiar, because you were never in the room when the decision happened. The agent was. You approved it. You own it. That's not the same thing as understanding it.

AI is stateless. It carries nothing between sessions. No memory of last month's outage. No scar tissue from the migration. No disrupted sleep. Even when memory is bolted on, it's a managed artifact, curated, maintained, carrying its own overhead. Not the embodied context that comes from having been there. AI performs engineering convincingly and then forgets. You carry all of it: the context no prompt can encode, the gut feeling about that module. Your context isn't a 200k token window. It's fifteen years of production bugs.

The friction I used to complain about was the ground floor. Not all of it was valuable. But it hid a dependency we didn't know we had. Writing code slowly meant understanding what you wrote. Sitting with a problem meant it changed shape in your mind; you noticed the edge case, you talked to a teammate. You couldn't generate your way past that.

I tried. Wrote thousands of lines of system prompts encoding what I knew. But encoding narrows. What you know lives in reflex, in pattern recognition you can't articulate. The prompt captures the conclusion but not the instinct that produced it. And it flattens: one engineer's history becomes the template, drowning out the judgment different histories produce.

Generation scales. Judgment doesn't.

<SectionDivider spacing="subsection" />

Use AI. Go deep with it. But don't use it to create things you'd never have created, owned, or maintained without it. The expense of creation filtered out the noise. That filter is gone. Now you are the filter. When creation was expensive, you didn't evaluate everything; you built what seemed sufficient and moved on. Cost gave you a stopping rule. Remove it and every decision stays open. You could have written that test. Was it the right one? The load isn't from producing more. It's from the unbounded obligation to decide what should exist.

The skill isn't prompting. It's knowing when to stop generating and start deciding what should exist. That capacity is finite. Better models won't reduce the burden. They'll increase it, because more convincing output demands sharper judgment. This isn't a phase. Capability and burden scale together. Not because the tools are immature, but because judgment is the bottleneck, and bottlenecks don't dissolve. They become more visible under load.

<SectionDivider spacing="subsection" />

The code is the truth. For now. It runs or it doesn't. That's more honest than anything above it.

Code is the most honest layer we have — and it's not honest enough. Too opaque to own at scale, too precise to replace with English. We were already losing ground-level understanding; no one person held a whole system. We're still looking for the layer where intent and execution meet clearly enough that a human can hold the whole thing.

<p class="closing">
  Until then, someone has to stand on the ground floor. That someone is you.
</p>
